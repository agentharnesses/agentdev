Follow-up to `2026-08-21-1203-consistency-and-cost-set-as-the-headline-measurement-priorities.md`, same session, hours later — the first real test of that decision, and it didn't hold up the way the pivot implied it might.

Ran `elo --times 5` (consistency-and-cost defaults: quality off, both consistency signals on) across three real question sets — `question-set-2` (fix a real bug), `question-set-3` (contextual questions), `question-set-4` (general summarization, new this session, a 6-step resumed continuum) — 45 total real continuum executions, 13 completed repeats per variant after `question-set-4` lost 2/5 to timeouts. Aggregated pooled across all 12 real step-observations:

- **Cost is a three-way wash.** 1.50M–1.57M total tokens per variant, within 5% of each other. Same shape as every single-question-set cost finding this project has produced today — `full-harness`'s cheap-looking real work consistently offset by its priming tax — now confirmed holding across three different question sets, not an artifact of one.
- **The two consistency signals disagree with each other at the aggregate level, not just as per-run noise.** LLM-judge picks `harness-removed` most consistent overall (1.75 avg rank); file-touch picks `metaskill-removed` (1.58). They agree closely on `question-set-3` (both pick `metaskill-removed`) but diverge sharply on `question-set-2` (file-touch: `full-harness` wins every single step, rank 1.00 flat; LLM-judge ties the other two ahead of it instead) and `question-set-4` (LLM-judge favors `harness-removed`; file-touch favors `metaskill-removed`, again at a flat 1.00). Two signals that were each independently validated as *directionally* meaningful (file-touch at +0.39 mean correlation against the LLM judge, specifically) still don't converge on the same answer once asked at question-set scale instead of single-comparison scale.

User's read, stated directly rather than something inferred: "everything seems to be a wash based on simplistic testing... there's no evidence that the harness does anything based on these tests, but anecdotally I feel like there is an impact in allowing a system to consistently understand complex queries, organize information effectively, and minimize the repetition of certain unwanted actions." Explicitly not concluding the standard does nothing — the hunch is that what it does is *hard to test with what's been built so far*, and that this gap (a real, felt effect that a simplistic test can't surface) is "increasingly common when integrating AI systems into solving real-world problems," not unique to this project.

Worth connecting to two things already on record rather than treating this as a fresh problem:

1. `2026-08-20-0057-wrong-turns-self-reveal-so-fixtures-need-silent-traps.md` made almost the identical point five months of diary-time ago, one level down: every decoy in the original hand-built fixtures self-revealed as wrong on inspection, so blind search barely paid for its wrong turns, and the actual failure mode routing exists to prevent (confidently landing on a wrong answer that never announces itself) was never actually being tested. The real-repo suites replaced hand-built fixtures with real content specifically to get past that — but the *questions* asked of that real content may have the same shape problem one level up: "answer this contextual question" and "summarize this repo" don't obviously stress "consistently understand complex queries" or "minimize repetition of unwanted actions" the way a harder, more adversarial task might.
2. Today's own arc (`0811` doubting `end_accuracy` → `0816`/`0842` building quality+consistency Elo → `1142`/`1159` validating two consistency signals against each other → `1203` committing to consistency-and-cost as the priority) already reflects one full cycle of "the metric doesn't discriminate, try a different metric." This entry is evidence that cycle isn't finished — swapping *what* gets measured (accuracy → consistency) didn't fix a wash if the *tasks* themselves aren't shaped to make a harness's real advantage show up at all.

Not a decision — "back to the drawing board on how this testing is handled" is the user's own framing, and nothing here proposes a specific redesign. Candidates worth having next time, named but not chosen: harder/longer tasks specifically designed to need cross-file organization or repeated-context retention (closer to what the anecdotal feel is actually about than a single contextual Q&A); tasks that penalize repeated unwanted actions directly (the anecdote explicitly names this, and nothing measured today targets it at all); or accepting that this standard's value may be real but not the kind of thing a same-repo, same-model comparison harness can isolate, and that the harness itself is a different kind of artifact than a benchmark target.
