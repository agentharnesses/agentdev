Follow-up to `2026-08-21-0848-comparison-selection-made-adaptive-not-fixed-schedule.md`, same session. Asked to read a specific paper and choose a pairing algorithm from it: Nagle et al., "Optimal Pairwise Comparison Procedures for Subjective Evaluation" (arXiv:2508.17840).

The paper compares round-robin, uniform random sampling, knockout tournaments, tree selection, a Bayesian information-gain method (Hybrid-MST), and its own proposal, **Sort-MST**, for ranking many competitors from as few pairwise comparisons as possible. Sort-MST wins on ranking accuracy while staying cheaper than the Bayesian method, and — the detail that mattered most here — the Bayesian method can actually *fail* under high judge uncertainty, producing imbalanced designs; Sort-MST doesn't have that failure mode. Mechanism: each round, compute a minimum spanning tree over all competitors with edge weight = current rating difference (a Bradley-Terry-grounded choice — comparisons are most informative when their outcome is least certain, i.e. ratings close together); the tree's `n-1` edges are that round's comparisons. Repeat, re-fitting ratings between rounds. Tested up to 15x a full round-robin's worth of comparisons, with accuracy still improving past a single pass — repeats of still-close pairs are intended, not waste.

This directly replaced `elo.py`'s `_next_pair`/`compared`-set design from the entry above, which was already adaptive but ad hoc: least-played-first, closest-rating tie-break, and explicitly forbade repeating a pair within one budget. Sort-MST is better grounded (closest-rating is now the *primary* signal, not a tiebreaker, matching the actual Bradley-Terry information-maximization argument) and simpler (the spanning-tree structure gets full-coverage-per-round for free from graph connectivity, no separate "games played" bookkeeping needed) — and it explicitly wants repeats across rounds, which the prior design deliberately prevented. Real behavior change: `_comparison_budget` is no longer capped at full round-robin (`C(n,2)`) — the paper's own tested range goes to 15x that — so the same `--comparisons-per-item` default (6) now spends more real judge calls than before at a given pool size.

22 tests (`test_elo.py`), including direct tests of `_spanning_tree_edges` (n-1 edges, full coverage, closest pair chosen first) and a test confirming repeats now happen by design when ratings don't move. 120/120 total suite passes. Not re-verified with a fresh live run on top of the earlier one — this stayed a targeted change to pure graph/loop logic behind an unchanged judge-calling interface, and the unit tests directly exercise the properties that changed; judged the same way as the previous entry's tradeoff.
