Follow-up to `2026-08-21-1340-a-wash-even-on-consistency-and-cost-methodology-in-doubt-not-the-standard.md`, same session. Asked directly why the standard doesn't show more impact than the anecdotal feel says it should; walked through five candidate reasons with varying evidence behind them (priming's fixed tax structurally offsetting routing's variable savings, cold search already near-optimal on `agentdev`'s own small tidy repo, nothing measured targets "minimizing repetition of unwanted actions" at all, `n=5` being thin against real noise, routing *quality* never varied independently of routing presence). Two of those — the self-referential, too-tidy test repo, and the total absence of a hard, non-LLM-judged ground truth — pointed at the same fix: external, real datasets with real answer keys. Asked what's actually out there.

Surveyed live (web search, not memory, given how fast this landscape moves and how much a wrong specific would cost here):

- **SWE-bench** — 2,294 real Issue→PR pairs across 12 large, popular Python repos (django, sympy, scikit-learn, matplotlib, etc.), ground truth is *execution-based*: the actual merged fix plus its fail-to-pass unit tests, not an LLM judge or a checklist. `SWE-bench Verified` is the human-vetted subset. This is the strongest single candidate for two reasons at once: the repos are large and genuinely messy (unlike `agentdev`, where cold `grep`/`find` already wins), and it's the only option here that removes LLM-judge uncertainty from correctness entirely — a real, objective pass/fail signal this project has never had access to.
- **RepoProbe** (Tencent-Hunyuan, ASE 2026) — already assessed once, `2026-08-20-2334`. 500 open-ended "how"/"why" questions from real GitHub Discussions across 50 repos, checklist-graded. Best fit for the *comprehension of complex queries* half of the original hunch specifically, since the questions are real users asking real maintainers to explain something — closer in spirit to this project's own question sets than SWE-bench's bug-fixing framing is. That earlier entry's verdict stands: don't fork the harness, pull the (repo, question, checklist) triples directly for a pilot.
- **CodeRepoQA** — 585,687 QA entries across 5 languages, notably *multi-turn* (avg 6.62 dialogue turns per entry), ground truth is the real maintainer's final reply. Strongest fit for testing whether routing's advantage *compounds* over a longer exchange — something today's mostly single-shot question sets (question-set-3's `resume_previous: false` on every step) never gave it room to show.
- Named more briefly, not yet read closely enough to rely on: **SWE-QA** (repo-level QA specifically, worth reading the paper before trusting size/structure claims from a search snippet alone) and **StackRepoQA** (preserves original developer-authored questions/accepted answers rather than LLM-rephrased ones, avoiding a synthetic-question bias risk the checklist-style benchmarks don't fully escape).

Recommendation given, not yet acted on: SWE-bench first if only one gets picked, specifically because it's the only candidate that also fixes the "quality is hard to judge" problem this whole session has circled — real repos, real complexity, and a ground truth that doesn't need an LLM judge or a checklist to adjudicate. RepoProbe second, for the comprehension-of-complex-queries angle SWE-bench's bug-fixing framing doesn't cover. Both eventually, ideally — they test different halves of the original anecdotal claim, not the same thing twice.
